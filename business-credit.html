Foundations a business can actually borrow on

Most small businesses are denied business credit for clerical reasons long before a lender reads a financial statement: the entity lists a home address, the business phone is a personal cell, the email is a free Gmail account, and the EIN name does not match the state filing character-for-character. The business credit track fixes the foundations in order — the same discipline its companion guide teaches (Guide #5, Business Credit Foundations).

  1. Entity & record hygiene audit — name, address, phone and email consistency across the Secretary of State, the IRS record, the bank and every registry
  2. D-U-N-S and bureau files — establishing the Dun & Bradstreet file and checking Experian Business / Equifax Business
  3. Net-30 vendor sequencing — starter vendor accounts that report, in the order they should be opened
  4. Business banking discipline — a dedicated account, no-NSF hygiene, and the paper trail lenders actually ask for

This is advice and organisation, sold as its own engagement. It is not funding brokering: no one can guarantee an approval, a limit or a rate, and any statement about funding in our materials is framed as lender discretion. Business-credit work for a client whose personal file we are also repairing is a separate engagement with separate paper.

The honest fine print — up front

You can dispute credit report errors yourself, for free, without paying any company — the federal AnnualCreditReport.com site and the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau explain how. No credit repair company, including Fiscally Fit, can remove accurate, current, verifiable information from a credit report, and no one can legally promise you a specific credit score, a deletion, or a date by which your credit will change. We sell the work, the organisation and the follow-through of disputing information you believe is inaccurate, incomplete or unverifiable — never an outcome. Fiscally Fit is a credit services organisation, not a law firm, tax advisor, mortgage lender or debt settlement company, and nothing on this site is legal, tax or investment advice.
